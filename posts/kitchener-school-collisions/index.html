Collisions near Kitchener schools

Pedestrian collisions in Kitchener fell steadily from 2015 to 2022, near schools and everywhere else, and about half happened where traffic controls were already in place.

Are the streets around Kitchener’s schools more dangerous than the rest of the city? This post looks at every reported collision in Kitchener from January 2015 to June 2022 and asks how many involved pedestrians, how many happened within 250 m of a school, and what kind of traffic control was in place where they happened.

The short answer: pedestrian collisions have been falling across the whole city, the area around schools is no exception, and about half of all pedestrian collisions happened at locations that already had a stop sign, traffic light, or crossing guard.

Pedestrian collisions fell by more than half

There were 231 collisions involving a pedestrian in the study period. The number per year fell steadily, from 42 in 2015 to 18 in 2021 (Figure 1). The 2022 figure covers only the first six months.

About half happened where traffic controls were in place

Just over half of all pedestrian collisions (129 of 231, or 56%) occurred at locations with some type of traffic control in place: traffic signals, stop signs, pedestrian crossovers, or crossing guards (Figure 2).

Collisions near schools follow the same trend

Of the 231 pedestrian collisions, 28 happened within 250 m of a school on a school day between 6 AM and 6 PM. (One of these was within 250 m of two schools, so the school-by-school counts in Table 1 add to 29.)

Comparing the area within 250 m of a school to the rest of the city, on regular school days (excluding the pandemic closure periods) between 6 AM and 6 PM, both show the same steady decline (Figure 3).

The pattern by traffic control also holds near schools: 15 of the 28 pedestrian collisions (54%) happened where some control was in place (Figure 4).

Most collisions near schools involve no pedestrian or cyclist

Table 2 counts every collision within 250 m of a school on a school day between 6 AM and 6 PM, with pedestrian and cyclist collisions broken out. The vast majority, about 95%, involved neither.

Time of day and type of day

Table 3 breaks the collisions near schools down by the type of day (regular school day, pandemic lockdown school day, or non-school day) and the time of day.

Because the three types of day are not equally common, Table 4 shows the same counts as a rate per day.

Map of collisions near schools

The map in Figure 5 shows the number and type of collisions within 250 m of each Kitchener school on regular school days between 6 AM and 6 PM. Hover over a circle for the school name and click it for the collision counts. A circle with a number groups several nearby schools; click it to zoom in.

Data and methods

  • Collisions come from the City of Kitchener open data portal and cover January 2015 to June 2022. Each record has a location, date and hour, the type of traffic control at the location, and whether a pedestrian or cyclist was involved.
  • Schools are the public (Waterloo Region District School Board) and Catholic (Waterloo Catholic District School Board) schools in Kitchener, geocoded from their published addresses.
  • Near a school means within 250 m of a school’s geocoded address, measured as straight-line distance.
  • School day means a weekday outside July and August. Pandemic closure periods (March to June 2020, late December 2020 to early February 2021, mid-April to June 2021) are treated separately as lockdown school days.
